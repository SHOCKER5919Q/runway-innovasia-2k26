# Predicted state model

*Companion to `THREE_STATE_ARCHITECTURE.md`. The mathematics is in
`DIGITAL_TWIN_MATHEMATICS.md`; the model training is in `ML_METHODOLOGY.md`.*

## What it is

An evidence-based estimate of the next 13 weeks, built from the actual state. It is
not a verified financial fact, and the product never renders it as one.

## Why it is ledger-driven rather than fitted

An MSME's next quarter is dominated by things that are already known: a few hundred
open invoices with due dates, a payroll run on the 2nd, GST on the 20th, an EMI on
the 7th, and supplier bills with contractual terms. Fitting a curve to past bank
balances throws all of that away and rediscovers a blurred version of it.

So the structure is additive and explicit:

```
closing[w] = closing[w−1]
           + open receivables predicted into week w       ← invoice level
           + sales not yet invoiced                        ← estimated
           − open payables due in week w                   ← invoice level
           − purchases for forecast sales                  ← estimated
           − obligations due in week w                     ← scheduled
           − recurring operating costs                     ← estimated
```

Statistical estimation is used where it belongs — the *unknown* components. For
sales not yet invoiced:

```
base    = median of the last 12 de-seasonalised weekly totals
trend   = median(last 4) / median(weeks 5–12), clipped to [0.80, 1.20]
week k  = base × seasonality[month(k)] × (1 + (trend − 1)·e^(−k/8))
```

The exponential damping matters: a four-week trend extrapolated thirteen weeks is
not a forecast, it is an extrapolation artefact.

## Components, labelled by their evidence

| Component | Evidence | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Open receivables | invoice-level, verified | The ledger, with a predicted payment date per invoice |
| Open payables | invoice-level, verified | The ledger, at the earlier of contractual and statutory due date |
| Obligations | scheduled, mixed | Payroll and rent are contractual; GST is a RUNWAY estimate and says so |
| Loan EMIs | contractual | The loan schedule |
| Sales not yet invoiced | **estimated** | De-seasonalised robust median with a damped trend |
| Purchases for those sales | **estimated** | COGS ratio from the industry profile, lead-shifted |
| Recurring operating costs | **estimated** | Trailing mean of non-invoice operating debits |

The Cash Runway screen shows this breakdown with the evidence class for each, so the
owner can see how much of a given week rests on a model rather than a document.

## Versioning

Every forecast is written as an immutable `PredictedSnapshot` plus one
`ForecastPoint` row per horizon week and one `PaymentPrediction` row per open
invoice. Those flattened rows are what later get resolved against actuals.

```
PredictedSnapshot
  baseline_actual_state_id    which reality it was built from
  model_version               "pdm-gbq-conformal-1.2.0|cashflow-mc-1.3.0"
  seed, horizon_weeks, method
  assumptions_json            every parameter, including ρ and its source
  series_json                 the full simulation result
```

## Reading the quantiles

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P10** | A pessimistic path. 10% of simulated futures are worse. **This is the one to plan against.** |
| **P50** | The median. |
| **P90** | An optimistic path. |

Runway is **censored** at the horizon: a runway of 13.0 weeks on a 13-week forecast
means "beyond the forecast window", not exactly thirteen. The API returns a
`censored` flag and the interface says so rather than printing a precise-looking
number that is not one.

## Measured accuracy

The product does not claim accuracy; it measures it. See `EVALUATION_RESULTS.md`,
regenerated by `./run.sh evaluate`. Where nothing has matured, the product reports
that nothing has matured.

## Known limitations

1. **Long-horizon coverage is below target** and measured on very few observations.
   Reported with its `n`.
2. **Horizon truncation is corrected for the scenarios it affects.** A 13-week
   window sees the cash cost of a revenue change before its collection effect, so
   run-rate-changing scenarios are re-run over 26 weeks and both figures are shown.
   13 weeks remains the primary horizon because it is the one an owner can act
   within. The limitation is still disclosed under `horizon_truncation_note` for
   the baseline forecast itself.
3. **No inventory**, so a stock build-up or run-down is invisible to the forecast.
4. **One industry profile per business.** A business straddling two models gets the
   closer of the two.
